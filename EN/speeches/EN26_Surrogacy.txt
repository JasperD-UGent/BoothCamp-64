Today I will talk about surrogacy. Surrogacy is often sold as a beautiful thing - a gift by which a woman enables other people to have the child they longed for and cannot conceive or carry themselves. 

But I will show you today reality is different. I will show you it puts the surrogate mother at risk and it can lead to human trafficking and therefore surrogacy should be avoided. 

First of all, what is surrogacy?

Surrogacy is a situation where a woman becomes pregnant and gives birth to a baby or babies for another person or a couple in a planned arrangement.

We have to distinguish two types of surrogacy.

The first is where the pregnant woman is the full biological mother, with the child conceived using her own egg, and therefore it's called "genetic" surrogacy.

The second is where the pregnant woman is not the genetic mother and the child is conceived using the egg of a different woman, and therefore it's called "gestational surrogacy".

In this case an embryo or embryos is being transferred into the uterus of a woman who has agreed to carry and give birth to the child using in vitro fertilisation. Nowadays this is also the most common form of surrogacy arrangement.

But surrogacy is not all sunshine and lollipops. First of all, there are severe health risks. Research in fact shows us that pregnant women in gestational surrogacy arrangements have a 7.8% chance of severe maternal complications, which is more than three times the rate of those who became pregnant naturally and almost twice the rate among those who got pregnant through IVF. 

These risks included postpartum haemorrhage, so losing excessive amounts of blood following the birth, severe pre-eclampsia, which means the women experience high blood pressure associated with pregnancy and serious postpartum infections. And also, the baby is at risk, because there is also a higher risk of the baby being born preterm, before 37 weeks of pregnancy.

Why are these risks higher? The risks seem to be higher because the baby is genetically unrelated to the woman. Let me explain this: pregnancy has a strong impact on the immune system. During pregnancy, women's immune systems are altered so they do not reject the growing baby.

Having a baby with different genetic material may affect a woman's immune response during pregnancy and increase the risk of complications. 

Another reason to take into account is the fact that in surrogacy multiple birth is quite common and there are increased risks linked to multiple births for women and babies. 

But health issues is only one of the problems regarding surrogacy. The major issue is another one: cross-border surrogacy arrangements can lead to human trafficking and the sale of children, especially in countries where there is no regulation of surrogacy.

Globally, there are three principal approaches taken in relation to the practice of surrogacy. The first approach, taken by Germany, France, and China, is that of banning surrogacy completely. The second approach, followed by Israel, Russia, Georgia, and Ukraine, is to make surrogacy completely legal, regardless of whether it is commercial or altruistic. The third approach is to allow only altruistic surrogacy and ban commercial surrogacy. For altruistic surrogacy, the surrogate mother gives birth to a child without payment, in principle, but she can be paid for and only for necessary expenses. In many countries including the UK, Australia, Canada, Hong Kong, South Africa, Greece, and Iceland, only altruistic surrogacy is permitted, while commercial surrogacy is prohibited.

And then of course, as always, there is a whole list of countries where this surrogacy thing is not regulated at all. These are the places where we see fertility clinics and surrogacy agencies engage in what I can only call illegal surrogacy arrangements. The intended parents come from EU and non-EU countries and pay large amounts of money to these shady agencies for their services. What these parents don't know – or maybe it is better to say they don't want to know – is that behind these agencies there are organized crime groups forcing women to become a surrogate mother. 

India for instance is one of the countries where people go to because there are many surrogate mothers available. Surrogacy tourism they call it. You can even find surrogacy farms. Lots of women living together, and they are all expecting a child for another couple. It is all being organized by the so-called surrogacy brokers. 

These brokers often justify surrogacy tourism saying that not only mother and the clinic but the whole surrounding area receives clear financial benefits from this kind of tourism. The women receive a substantial sum of money after nine months and this money will not only improve their lives but enables their family to have better options in the future. And also the area gains from it: the intended parents often have an extended stay while undergoing the IVF procedure at the beginning of the process, and return to collect the newborn baby after the delivery. Local hotels and businesses benefit from this kind of tourism.

But can we really say women in such poor countries really make a well-considered choice to become a surrogate mother? I actually doubt that. 

Because having a child of your own is often a dream, a blessing people desperately search for. But not at any cost.

Ladies and gentlemen.

Today, I would like to discuss a very serious topic with you. One that we simply cannot sweep under the rug. It is an issue that affects children all over the world. Unfortunately, it's not a light-hearted topic that will make you smile or fill your heart with joy. I'm afraid it's not good news.

Today's topic is child marriage. But before we delve deeper, I will first explain the differences between child marriages, forced marriages, and arranged marriages. Then I will discuss where child marriages are most likely to take place and what causes them.

First of all, let's define the term 'child marriage'. A child marriage is any formal marriage or informal union in which one or both partners are under the age of 18. This definition focuses solely on the age of the bride and groom. This is why child marriage and forced marriage are different: in a forced marriage, one or both people do not consent to the marriage, and therefore pressure or abuse is used. This can include threats, physical or sexual violence, and financial pressure. Methods that leave deep scars.

It is important to remember that forced marriages can affect anyone, regardless of their background or nationality, and their gender. Unlike child marriage, forced marriage does not only affect young people. Adults can be victims too. No one is off-limits.

A third phenomenon that I should mention is arranged marriage, where the couple are not involved in planning or agreeing the marriage. In such marriages, the spouses are typically chosen by parents, families, community elders, matchmakers or religious leaders. A fundamental element of an arranged marriage is that the couple has agreed to the union. In other words, both parties have the right to refuse the spouse matched with them.

Now that you know the differences between these three types of marriage, it will not come as a surprise that all child marriages are forced. After all, no child can provide informed consent. Therefore, all child marriages violate children's rights.

Child marriage mainly impacts girls, but boys can experience this harmful practice too, albeit less frequently. For simplicity's sake, I will use the term 'child brides' today, even though we are not only dealing with brides.

Child brides can be found in every region of the world, including Africa, the Middle East, Latin America, South Asia and Europe. The problem knows no borders. Globally, 20.7% of young women, that is to say currently aged between 20 and 24, were married as children.

Even in Western Europe and, more specifically, the UK, 1,400 suspected cases of forced child marriage were detected last year. 

However, child marriage is most prevalent in sub-Saharan Africa, particularly in countries such as Niger and the Central African Republic, where it accounts for 76% and 68.5% of marriages, respectively.

In many communities across these regions, girls are being violently abducted and compelled to marry their often much older captors.

Worldwide, more than 250 million women alive today were married before their 15th birthday, many against their will. 15 million girls are pressured into marriage each year. That's a shocking 28 weddings a minute. 

Let's have a closer look now at those child marriages and the main causes of this phenomenon. First of all, it should be noted that most countries have laws against the union of minors. Unfortunately, various exceptions to the minimum age requirement undermine these laws and make them quite difficult to enforce. Exceptions include parental consent, authorization of the court or religious laws.

The reasons for child marriage differ from country to country. In sub-Saharan Africa, however, where the practice is most widespread, the primary drivers are gender inequality and poverty. 

Let's look at both aspects in more detail. First of all, gender inequality. In many countries, particularly in Africa, there are deeply rooted beliefs that value boys over girls. Communities expect girls to become wives and mothers. There is a strong desire to control women. Their wings are clipped before they even learn to fly. Younger wives are also considered preferable as they are expected to be more obedient and more likely to be virgins.

The other root cause is poverty. In Africa, for example, adolescent females from the poorest households are twice as likely to marry before their 18th birthday as those from the richest families. Similarly, girls in rural areas are twice as likely to become child brides as those in cities.

For poor families with many children, marrying off a daughter early can mean one less mouth to feed. Additionally, there is a financial aspect due to dowry and bride price traditions. Receiving money or animals from the groom's family simply significantly increases the family's income.

Now, it will be crystal clear to all of you that a child marriage is an act of violence and a violation of minors' rights. It robs them of their childhood, their education and their independence, and prevents them from choosing their own future. 

So, let's all work together to end child marriage. We must change the mindset of local communities, bring perpetrators to justice and campaign to influence policies to stop this kind of violence. Ultimately, education, not marriage, is the key to a child's successful future.

Thank you for your attention.

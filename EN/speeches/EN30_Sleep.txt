Ladies and gentlemen.

I'll start my speech today with a fun fact: did you know we spend a third of our lives sleeping? 24 years with our eyes closed, apparently doing nothing. From an evolutionary point of view, it does not seem like a very smart strategy. You will all know the expression "the survival of the fittest". Now, when animals are asleep they are definitely not the fittest, but instead they are vulnerable to predators. Yet no living creature can do without rest. Sleep must serve some purpose, but what exactly? Scientists are still on the fence about the exact purpose of sleep.

Some believe that when we sleep all the toxins accumulated during the day are being washed away and the brain is spick-and-span again to tackle another day in life. In fact, if these substances are not properly disposed of, they end up accumulating and can contribute to the onset of neurodegenerative diseases, such as Alzheimer's disease. This preventive cleaning seems to work best when the brain is not engaged in daytime activities.

Another theory is that sleep serves to consolidate memories. In other words, it reinforces the most important concepts we have learned during the day and discards those that are unnecessary to remember. While you sleep, your memory is literally being refreshed. That is also why the word that was on the tip of your tongue the day before is back after a good night's sleep.

But sleeping is not only beneficial to finally get a memory like an elephant. While you are sleeping like a log, your body is producing substances that are very important for the organism, such as growth hormones and certain proteins that serve to repair tissues. The fact is that when we do not get enough sleep or act like night owls who don't need much sleep, the effects are quickly noticeable. And the problem of insufficient sleep is a global problem – an estimated 32.8 % of adults do not get enough sleep. 14.5% of adults have trouble falling asleep and 17.8% of adults have trouble staying asleep. There's also a gender difference – women are more likely to have trouble falling asleep than men. We are talking 17.1% of women compared to 11.7% of men.

Just one sleepless night is enough to feel the first negative effects on mood and concentration. If this lack of sleep increases, your language skills, memory, and reflexes in response to rapidly changing situations are affected. After 17 hours without sleep, there is a decline in cognitive performance similar to that which would occur after two large glasses of wine. Furthermore, chronic sleep deprivation can lead to cardiovascular problems, obesity, diabetes, and certain types of cancer. Let it be a reason to hit the sack early at night.

But how much sleep is enough? How much do we really need? In general, sleep is a very active process, in which the brain is anything but at rest. For that reason, adult humans need an average of 8 hours of sleep to lead a healthy life? Yet most of them are not getting there. Adults clock an average of 6.8 hours of sleep on weekdays, while on weekends it is 7.8 hours. Things also don't appear to be improving with time, as 44% of adults also feel that their sleep has deteriorated over the past 5 years.

It's important to know that as we grow older, we sleep less, perhaps due to a reduction in the number of brain cells that "tell" the brain when to sleep. The quality of sleep also declines with age. That's why children go out like a light after one single bedtime story and sleep tight all night long. Adults, on the other hand, often have to count hundreds of sheep in order to fall asleep and then spend the night tossing and turning in bed. And then, the next morning, they are wide-awake before their alarm clock tells them it is time to get up.

So let me finish my speech today by giving you some tips and tricks, especially for those who struggle to fall asleep once they hit the pillow. Putting away your smartphone just before going to sleep is key. I won't go too much into detail but the bottom line is that the blue light from electronic devices inhibits the production of melatonin. That is the sleep hormone that will make you fall asleep. It also helps to create a relaxing bedtime routine to signal to your body that it is time to sleep. Examples are reading, taking a warm bath, or practicing deep breathing exercises. It is better not to engage in physically heavy sport activities late in the evening because it can interfere with your ability to fall asleep. It is absolutely true that regular daily exercise improves sleep quality, but avoid intense workouts within 2 to 3 hours of bedtime. It is better to do this kind of activity before dinner and then flop on the sofa to start unwinding.

So, my conclusion today is: put your feet up in the evening and you will sleep like a baby. And if that doesn't work for you, feel free to take a cat nap during the day. It will also recharge your energy level and boost your perfomance and your mood. 

And for tonight I wish you all sweet dreams.

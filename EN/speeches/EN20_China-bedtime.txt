Dear colleagues, I don't know what time you usually go to bed. Do you go to bed early to make sure you get enough sleep, so you can rise and shine, so that you can get up bright and early the next day? Or are you more of a night owl? 

I'm a bit of an early bird myself, and that's because for years, I had to get up at 5.30 due to a long commute. So you might expect that since I get up early, I also go to bed early. Well, sometimes, but not always, unfortunately. You see, I tend to go through periods of insomnia, so I guess I'm both an early bird and a night owl. And whenever I talk about this with friends and colleagues, I have the impression the number of other people who are also up in the dead of night is only increasing. 

Just the other day, I came across an article about this precise topic, burning the candle at both ends: going to bed late and getting up early. I'll tell you a bit more about the context and maybe you can relate to it as well.

The article was specifically about China. You see, many Chinese workers have been forced to adopt the so-called '996 schedule'. This notorious 996 schedule means that they work from nine in the morning to nine in the evening, and they do so six days a week. After their shift, which sometimes also includes overtime, they have a narrow window of time to eat, shower and hit the sack. A recent report says the average Chinese employee only has about 2.5 hours a day when they are not at work or asleep.

Two and a half hours to yourself, during which you also need to eat, clean, run errands, etc. I think you'll agree with me, that's not a life. A few hours of time to yourself before going to bed is necessary to survive. And so the 996 has led to a reaction among workers: they sacrifice sleep to eke out some personal time. Often, they will stay up surfing the internet, reading the news and watching online videos until well after midnight.

The Chinese even have a term for this. In English, this phenomenon is translated to 'revenge bedtime procrastination'. In other words: people who don't have much control over their daytime life refuse to go to sleep early in order to regain some sense of freedom during late-night hours.

The phrase may originate in China, but the phenomenon it describes is probably widespread. Over-stretched workers all over the world are putting off bedtime to claim some precious personal time. The result, of course, is we're not getting enough sleep, even though we know it's not good for us. Indeed, experts have long warned that insufficient sleep is a global public-health epidemic. Let me give you some figures. 

I'll start with a survey which was carried out in 2019. It received more than 11,000 responses from people in 12 countries. This survey showed that 62% of adults worldwide feel they don't get enough sleep. On average, they sleep 6.8 hours on a weeknight compared to the recommended number of 8 hours. People cited various reasons for this shortfall – I won't mention all of them – but I will say that 37% blamed their lack of sleep on their hectic work or school schedule.

I'd like to discuss another study as well. This one is exclusively about China. This Chinese national survey shows that 60% of people born after 1990 are not getting enough sleep. It also shows that those living in the biggest cities suffer the most. The reason for that is that the tech companies who created the 996 culture tend to be based in big cities. But just to be clear, this isn't a problem limited to the tech industry. Their work practices have influenced other sectors too. 

Dear colleagues, it's understandable people resent work squeezing their leisure time, but reducing sleep is probably not the best response. Sleep deprivation, especially long term, can lead to a host of harmful effects, both mental and physical. Sleep deprivation is linked to a higher risk of stroke, diabetes and obesity. It weakens the immune system, causes hormonal imbalances, etc. etc. The list of potential health issues is long. 

So why do people stay up late for some free time, even though they know it's bad for their health? That's because relaxing after work is really important. After all, all work and no play makes Jack a dull boy, right? If we don't take a break from work stress, it can hurt our wellbeing and lead to burnout. Sleep helps us recover, but to sleep well, we first need to fully switch off from work. That's why people trade sleep for downtime – they need that mental break.

Dear colleagues, earlier on I said maybe you can relate to this topic of sleep deprivation. Well, to be honest, I hope you can't. I hope you're not counting sheep all too often. I honestly hope you have a healthier sleep pattern. I hope you get a solid 8 hours a night, sleeping like a log.

Thank you for your attention.

Ladies and gentlemen.

I don't know about you, but I do have some favourite quotes. You know, like the ones you hang on your fridge printed on a magnet. One of my fridge magnets says: "There's nothing better than a good friend, except a good friend with chocolate". The one below says: "Life is a journey, and chocolate makes the ride a little sweeter". And recently I bought one that says: "All you need is love. But a little chocolate now and then doesn't hurt".

You will get it by now. I am a die-hard chocolate lover. Especially dark chocolate is my cup of tea. And I salve my conscience saying that dark chocolate is loaded with nutrients that positively affect my health. In fact, if you buy quality dark chocolate with a high cocoa content, then it's quite nutritious. It contains a decent amount of soluble fibre and is loaded with minerals.

A 50-gram bar of dark chocolate with 70% to 85% cocoa contains approximately 5.5 grams of fibre, 33% of the daily value of iron and 28% of the daily value of magnesium. 

In addition, it has plenty of potassium and zinc. And now you will say, but chocolate also contains fats. And that is true. But most of them are good fats, with a neutral effect on body cholesterol.

Of course, 50 grams is a fairly large amount, as these nutrients and healthy fats also come with 300 calories and 12 grams of sugar. For this reason, I have to admit that dark chocolate is best consumed in moderation.

And even though Forest Gump said that "life is like a box of chocolates. You never know what you're gonna get". I know exactly what I am going to get. I get a powerful source of antioxidants that help my body fight against free radicals which may increase the risk of developing diseases. These antioxidants play a role in lowering LDL, "bad" cholesterol, preventing cell damage, boosting brain health et cetera. 

So sometimes I think we should change the 4 layers of the typical food pyramid into 4 basic food groups for chocolate lovers. First level: milk chocolate. Second level: dark chocolate. Third level: white chocolate. And the last layer: chocolate truffles.

But all jokes aside, you don't need to be one smart cookie to know that dark chocolate isn't that bad at all for your health, on the contrary! Dark chocolate contains flavonoids which stimulate the arteries to produce nitric oxide. One of the functions of nitric oxide is to send signals to the arteries to relax, which lowers the resistance to blood flow and therefore reduces blood pressure.

So when I take out a bar of chocolate as a little sweet treat for myself, I tell myself I am simply taking care of myself. In that way it is no longer a guilty pleasure but just some me time to stay as fit as a fiddle. And the nicest thing is that I can even be a couch potato while working on my heart health. It's really a piece of cake!

However, it's not all sunshine and lollypops. Because research is mixed. A 2019 study in women found that eating 85% dark chocolate slightly elevated blood pressure levels 2 hours after eating. That said, eating dark chocolate during periods of stress actually helped lower blood pressure and heart rate slightly.

Given the great variation between studies on this subject, more research is needed to understand the effects of eating dark chocolate on blood pressure. But for the time being, I will give the benefit of the doubt to those researchers that found that my stress levels can be managed by eating some chocolate.

For me this could be the difference between life and death. Heart diseases run in my family. I have to play it safe if I want to grow old. In my case, seen the family history of heart diseases, I might have a higher risk of developing conditions that can lead to a heart attack or stroke, so for me it is better to be safe than sorry. And eating chocolate will most definitely reduce this risk on developing a heart disease. In fact, the compounds in dark chocolate appear to be highly protective against the oxidation of LDL. In the long term, this may lead to less cholesterol lodging in the arteries, resulting in a lower risk of heart disease.

And that is exactly why I prefer to eat some pieces of chocolate as an afternoon snack or dessert after dinner. In that very moment I am protecting my heart and working on my health. 
And according to a study from 2017 I even have to do so not once but at least 3 times a week in order to lower the risk of cardiovascular disease by 9%.

This outcome was also confirmed by a study from 2018 that suggested that that eating 45 grams of chocolate per week lowers cardiovascular disease risk by 11%. 45 grams of chocolate of course is peanuts for someone like me, I often exceed that amount but hey, there is no harm in doing your heart lots of favours.

It's like a workout handed to you on a silver platter. How good can life get?

So, ladies and gentlemen, my conclusion today is, do yourself and your health a favour and follow the 12-step chocolate program: never be more than 12 steps away from chocolate!
